## All-rule challenge ledger

L references below are the private `sources/JAxx.md` lines. Tiny quotations are evidence fragments; surrounding prose, quotes and later revisions were checked. Each decision applies to the reviewed skill, not a supposed universal grammar law.

### R1 register — accept with editorial-status qualification

Support: JA01 L10–14/L198–240 uses polite exercise narration and moves from commands to state; JA17 L128–140 uses plain technical explanation. Counterexample: JA05 L3 uses plain summary while L8 onward is polite; JA16's opening/closing polite summary surrounds extensive plain narrative. Thus whole-document `ですます` enforcement is rejected. Consistency per explanatory unit remains a reasonable editorial preference, not an empirically mandatory rule. New polite-document default is explicitly a project proposal, not existing Coppice policy proven here. Headings/UI labels/quotes remain separate units.

### R2 subjects and actors — accept, without grammatical overreach

Support: JA03 L144–166 repeatedly names Alice, reader and Bob, then carefully scopes inferred sameness. JA17 L152–165 contrasts parent and child process roles. Counterexample: JA01 L203–238 omits the reader subject in successive actions without confusion; inserting `あなたは` every time would be unnatural. JA03 L1189 actually says **「ボブは誰か？」** and warns Bob can change during discussion. The first-pass analysis's phrase “ボブって誰だよ” is not a verbatim passage and must not be quoted as one. A topic marked は does not bind every later predicate. Actor expansion must be supported, not an excuse to invent who has a permission.

### R3 clause/modifier structure — accept, strengthen condition/time-origin guard

Support: JA19 L22 nests duration, signal, nontermination and optional KillAfter; JA17 L137–140 lists lifecycle stages. Counterexamples: JA08 L40 and JA24 L177 are long but interpretable comparisons/modifiers. Neither brevity nor removal of every の is justified. Splitting may detach a condition or create cause from sequence. T03 exposed an additional real defect: `場合は`→`場合に限り` and an assumed timing origin can be false even in a fluent rewrite. Reviewed skill now names those dangers; no sentence-length quota.

### R4 nominalization/こと — accept as anti-absolutist editorial judgment

Support: JA03 L386–388 makes identity matching a proposition, and its explanatory scenarios repeatedly nominalize facts rather than actions. JA20 L27 explains reuse/testing as concepts. Counterexample to “make everything verbal”: JA07 L5 `開発の動機` is a useful nominal heading; its requirements list intentionally includes noun/adjective fragments. Confirmation and execution are distinct operations. The operation-shortening heuristic is an editor's technique, not proof that every noun form in the corpus is bad. Preserve useful こと and passives; no general noun ban.

### R5 tense/aspect/evidence — accept; conservative actor correction in T10

Support: JA05 L64 distinguishes verified resolution from unknown exact timing and forecast; JA29 L26–39 distinguishes unchanged throughput, a top impression and guesses. Counterexamples to uniform present or perfect certainty: JA07 L30/L115 has unimplemented migration, and JA24 L13/L205 has proposal/trial state. Do not smooth these into delivered features. “している” can denote ongoing activity or resultant state; T10 now keeps the backup **process** as topic rather than implying a new operator. Historical future statements are not current roadmaps. No example execution invented.

### R6 capability/permission/uncertainty — accept, not a demand to replace every できる

Support: JA03 L247–264 limits knowledge to a probable same person under explicit assumptions; JA16 L219 limits scale to reads. JA11 L221–224 explicitly leaves implementation uninvestigated. Counterexample to deleting hedges: JA23 L110–133 has suspected extension origin, unknown detail and narrowly checked cases. JA23 is 2026-updated live prose, not guaranteed 2017 wording. `できない` must not be changed to policy prohibition. Capability can remain naturally expressed as できる when contract context fixes the sense; clarification must not choose an unknown fact.

### R7 normative force — accept with required enrichment

Support: JA06 L25 appends individual confirmation to provider-update assumptions, L46 preserves the do-not-publish-restored-old-code warning; JA28 L66 offers an optional worker. Counterexamples to single-word force classification: JA20 L31 explicitly says case-dependent; JA32 L138 permits an inner function despite the categorical title, and L171 limits return behavior. A `必要だと思われる` statement is tentative necessity, not a recommendation or certain requirement. Reviewed skill now requires reading speaker, purpose, quotation, negation and hedging, not treating 必須/必要/推奨 as mechanically fixed tokens. Historical security advice is not endorsed.

### R8 logic/scope — accept after fixing T03 and T14

Support: JA04 L78 checks empty **or** unequal; JA19 L22 requires nontermination plus specified KillAfter before the described SIGKILL step; JA22 L75 separates reporting from blocking. Counterexamples: JA17 L214 makes a broad “no limit” claim immediately limited at L215; JA30's full-support bullet has Esper scope in its footnote. Thus “always put all limits in the same sentence” is not an observed corpus law, though safety-sensitive docs benefit from local limits. Important addition: sufficient condition is not exclusive necessary condition, and `記録するだけ` can negate more functionality than `遮断しない`. Revised tests remove both unsupported strengthenings.

### R9 terminology/literals — accept with typography/history labels

Support: JA12 L60–68 distinguishes syntax transformation from API polyfill; JA15 L116–120 has platform-specific quoting; JA21 L35 distinguishes -all from omitted flag. Counterexamples to mechanically uniform foreign-word handling: JA17 locally defines thread scope and JA16 redefines asynchronous in its particular context; English/code and Japanese explanatory terms serve different roles. JA12 explicitly rewrites naming in 2018; JA22 current safelist wording cannot be retrodated to 2016. Existing-file punctuation/spacing is editorial convention, not a linguistic consensus. Updating obsolete technical names may be warranted as a separate, verified technical change, not concealed in prose smoothing.

### R10 explanatory order/examples — accept by genre, not template law

Support: JA10/11 move through minimum demo and larger example; JA27 gives host environment, concrete retention reason and separate target setup; JA29 gives input conditions then negative result and hypotheses. Counterexample: JA25 L3/L332 says there is no tidy conclusion; JA26 is event-centered technical reflection; JA16 follows a learning history. No universal “answer first + three steps” or autobiographical voice requirement. UI/reference/README transfer is explicitly an editorial extrapolation because those genres were not independently sampled at scale. No invented output, measurement or product feature to fill a missing explanatory step.

### R11 punctuation/respect — accept only as contextual editorial guidance

Support: JA01 is polite and repeats essential names/actions; prose structure, not ornamental variation, carries clarity. Counterexamples: JA13 L39 and JA30 L40 are exuberant, JA27 L280 uses click shorthand; JA03 L1154 uses a courteous acknowledgement. A universal ban on emotion, repetition or polite forms would contradict the sample. No direct comparative evidence establishes that deleting every `させていただく` improves writing: ordinary-document simplification is a project editorial choice with contextual exceptions, not a discovered grammar law. Keep uncertainty/acknowledgement where meaningful. No forced typos, fake apologies, slang or single-writer mimicry.
